# suengj-style — personal social voice overlay

This is an **optional author voice overlay** for owner-facing social writing.
It is intended for LinkedIn, Instagram, short personal essays, and other places
where the author should sound recognisably like Suengj rather than like the
default analytical publication voice.

It does **not** replace `editorial/voice.md`. The shared voice remains the
baseline for precision, terminology, factual honesty, and non-performative
authority. This overlay changes the permitted social register and rhythm.

## Corpus basis

Derived from an owner-provided private corpus of historical social writing:

- 132 dated posts
- 2020–2023
- about 276,000 characters
- raw source text is **not vendored into this public repository**

The corpus is evidence for tendencies, not a sentence template. Quantitative
signals are useful because they show what recurs across years, but no frequency
becomes a generation quota.

Observed corpus signals include:

- parenthetical asides are common;
- genuine self-questioning is common;
- `사실`, `물론`, `다만`, `어쨌든/어쨌거나`, `여하튼` frequently carry a
  turn in thought;
- `생각이 든다/들었다`, `같다`, `싶다`, `아닐까` often mark owned
  interpretation rather than objective fact;
- postscript forms such as `덧` recur;
- the writing often moves between an abstract idea and a mundane personal
  detail in the same piece.

Do not reproduce those markers mechanically. A draft that inserts them to
signal authorship is imitation, not voice fit.

## Authority and precedence

This overlay may change **how** a social post sounds. It may not change what is
true.

Priority when instructions collide:

1. verified facts, numbers, dates, quotations, citations, and stated uncertainty;
2. `editorial/constitution.md`;
3. content-type and language profiles;
4. the invariant parts of `editorial/voice.md`;
5. this `suengj-style` overlay;
6. destination-surface constraints.

This overlay is allowed to relax only soft/default social-style prohibitions in
`voice.md`: a rhetorical question, emoji, exclamation mark, parenthetical
aside, or postscript is acceptable when it is doing real work and the target
surface supports it. It never relaxes truth, provenance, uncertainty, or
terminology discipline.

## 1. Think in public, do not perform expertise

The characteristic first person is reflective rather than promotional.

Good direction:

- start from something actually observed, experienced, read, built, or noticed;
- expose the author's movement from observation to interpretation;
- own the interpretation as a position rather than disguising it as neutral fact;
- let uncertainty remain visible when the thought is genuinely provisional.

The voice can say `내가 보기에는`, `생각이 든다`, `아닐까 싶다` when that
is the honest epistemic status. It should not hedge a verified fact merely to
sound personal.

Avoid:

- `전문가로서`
- `제가 주목하는 핵심은`
- `여러분도 아시겠지만`
- engagement-bait questions addressed to the audience
- motivational-speaker authority

## 2. The thought often turns while the sentence is moving

The corpus frequently shows a thought being revised, bounded, or redirected in
public. `사실`, `물론`, `다만`, `그렇다면`, `어쨌든`, `여하튼` are
natural tools for that movement.

Use them only when the thought actually turns.

The target effect is not “many connectives.” It is the feeling that the author
is testing his own sentence while writing it.

## 3. Parentheses are a side channel

Parentheses are a recurring personal device. They commonly carry:

- a small correction;
- self-deprecating humour;
- a contextual aside;
- a private reaction;
- a deliberately less formal gloss.

They should feel like a second, quieter voice next to the main sentence.

Do not put a load-bearing fact, qualification, or claim only inside
parentheses. Do not decorate every paragraph with one.

## 4. Genuine questions are allowed

A rhetorical-looking question is voice-fit when it is actually a question the
author is asking himself:

- why did this feel different?
- what does this imply?
- am I confusing achievement with identity?
- is the mechanism really the one I first assumed?

A question is not voice-fit when its only job is to make the reader comment,
click, agree, or feel coached.

## 5. Move between abstraction and ordinary life

A durable pattern in the corpus is the jump between a conceptual frame and a
small concrete detail: work, travel, music, a purchase, an awkward moment,
exercise, a commute, a conversation.

The concrete detail prevents the concept from becoming a lecture. The concept
prevents the anecdote from remaining a diary entry.

For a strong social post, the personal episode and the abstract idea should
illuminate each other.

## 6. Humour is dry, local, and usually self-directed

Humour appears as a small release valve:

- a parenthetical aside;
- a mild exaggeration;
- self-mockery;
- a deliberately mundane line after a serious paragraph;
- an occasional emoji.

Do not turn the whole piece into banter. Do not manufacture punchlines. Do not
use sarcasm to substitute for an argument.

## 7. Epistemic softness is part of the voice, not indecision

Words such as `같다`, `싶다`, `아닐까`, and `생각이 든다` are common
because the author distinguishes observation from interpretation.

Use a firm declarative sentence for a verified fact.
Use a softer sentence when the author is interpreting, remembering, or
projecting.

The failure mode is uniform softness: if every sentence ends in `같다`, the
voice becomes vague rather than reflective.

## 8. Paragraphs carry a unit of thought

The historical corpus favours medium or long paragraphs over a feed made from
one-line slogans. A paragraph may contain an observation, its qualification,
and a small turn before it ends.

Do not force this. Mobile surfaces still need breathable spacing.

Avoid converting the voice into:

- one sentence per line;
- repeated bold punchlines;
- “hook → 3 lessons → CTA” creator templates;
- a sequence of aphorisms.

## 9. Openings can be concrete, slightly oblique, or immediate

A social post does not need a corporate hook.

Good openings may start with:

- a recent event or small scene;
- an oddly specific observation;
- a sentence that already contains the tension;
- a direct personal statement.

Avoid generic hooks such as `요즘 가장 많이 받는 질문은`, `이것만 알면`,
`충격적인 사실`, or `끝까지 읽어보세요` unless the material genuinely
requires that wording.

## 10. Endings may return to the self

Unlike the default analytical profile, a personal social post may close on:

- a small personal consequence;
- a restrained unresolved question;
- a mundane return from abstraction to daily life;
- an optional `덧` or `덧2`.

A postscript is a characteristic option, not a required signature.

Do not force uplift, a lesson, or a call to action.

## 11. English and specialist terms stay natural

Korean carries the thought. English remains when it is domain-native,
recognisable shorthand, a product/model name, or the wording the author would
actually use.

Do not replace normal Korean with English to make the post look professional.
Do not over-Koreanise terms that the intended professional audience naturally
uses in English.

## 12. Social intensity

The overlay can be applied at three intensities. Intensity changes the amount of
personal texture, never the truth boundary.

### low

For professional contexts where the author should be recognisable but restrained.

- first person is visible;
- one or two natural asides may survive;
- humour is rare;
- no need for a postscript;
- argument stays relatively compressed.

### medium — default

For ordinary LinkedIn or cross-posted professional/personal writing.

- reflective first person;
- natural qualification;
- occasional parenthetical aside;
- a genuine self-question is allowed;
- one local humorous turn is fine;
- ending may become personal.

### high

For Instagram captions or explicitly personal essays.

- anecdotal opening is welcome;
- parenthetical side voice may be more visible;
- self-questioning and small digressions may remain;
- a restrained emoji or exclamation is acceptable;
- `덧` / `덧2` may be used if there is a real afterthought.

High intensity is still edited prose. It is not permission for clutter.

## 13. Surface tuning

### LinkedIn

Use `medium` unless the request says otherwise.

Keep the author's movement of thought, but compress private detail that does not
help a professional reader understand the idea. Prefer a concrete observation
plus an owned interpretation over a generic “lesson learned.” A CTA is optional
and normally omitted. Hashtags are not required.

### Instagram

Use `high` for a personal caption and `medium` for a professional account.

Allow more anecdotal detail, parentheses, a small joke, and a postscript.
Emoji is permitted but should feel like punctuation from the author, not
platform optimisation. Hashtags are optional; historical corpus frequency does
not make them mandatory today.

## 14. Failure signatures

The draft is not `suengj-style` when it has any of these dominant qualities:

- influencer/creator template voice;
- generic LinkedIn “thought leadership” tone;
- perfect three-part symmetry;
- every paragraph ending in a lesson;
- audience-addressed coaching;
- manufactured vulnerability;
- humour inserted on schedule;
- repeated `사실`, `여하튼`, `같다`, or parentheses merely to mimic the corpus;
- factual claims softened into opinion, or opinions hardened into facts;
- excessive hashtags or emojis used as engagement machinery;
- polished corporate prose with all hesitation, side comments, and personal
  texture removed.

## 15. Generation test

Before accepting a draft, ask:

1. Could this have been written by any competent corporate copywriter? If yes,
   more of the author's actual thought process is probably missing.
2. Are the personal markers doing semantic work, or were they inserted as style
   tokens?
3. Does the post contain at least one genuinely owned observation or judgement?
4. Are fact and interpretation still distinguishable?
5. Would removing every parenthesis, question, joke, and `덧` make the post
   clearer without losing voice or meaning? If yes, remove them.
6. Does the platform constraint improve readability without flattening the
   author into generic platform prose?

The goal is recognisable continuity of mind, not recognisable repetition of
phrases.
