# Korean language authorities registry

A registry of external bodies whose published material bears on Korean-language
correctness, plain-language practice, newsroom convention, institutional
report writing, audience/vocabulary level, or domain terminology. Governed by
the same rule as the rest of `references/`: URL, title, attribution, and our
notes only. **Never a mirrored copy of the referenced work.** See
`../editorial/RIGHTS-AND-PROVENANCE.md` §5 and `../docs/architecture/REPOSITORY-CONTRACT.md`.

This file is a pointer registry, not a rulebook. It records *what each
authority is authoritative for and what it is not*. The Korean rules
themselves — spacing, spacing exceptions, register, sentence construction —
live in the Korean language pack, derived from these sources but not
identical to any one of them.

Every record below shares one shape:

- **Source** — issuing body, work title, URL.
- **Authority class** — `NORMATIVE` (codified standard) / `STRONG_GUIDANCE`
  (official, advisory) / `EMPIRICAL_REFERENCE` (well-edited example material —
  evidence of craft, never of correctness) / `TERMINOLOGY_AUTHORITY`
  (canonical term list).
- **Governs** — the specific things it is authoritative for.
- **Does NOT govern** — explicit, mandatory, substantive.
- **Accessed** — 2026-09-05.
- **Rights note** — what we may store.

## 1. Korean normative language authorities

### 국립국어원 한국어 어문 규범 (National Institute of Korean Language — Korean Language Norms)

- **Source**: 국립국어원 (National Institute of Korean Language, under 문화체육관광부),
  "한국어 어문 규범" (한글 맞춤법, 표준어 규정/표준 발음법, 외래어 표기법, 국어의
  로마자 표기법). https://korean.go.kr/kornorms
- **Authority class**: NORMATIVE
- **Governs**: Orthography (한글 맞춤법), spacing rules stated within the
  orthography standard, standard pronunciation, standard-vs-dialect word
  status, loanword transliteration into Hangul, romanization of Korean.
  This is the codified standard the Korean language pack's mechanical rules
  (spacing, spelling, loanword form) trace back to.
- **Does NOT govern**: Sentence-level style, register, tone, structure,
  editorial voice, or anything about what makes writing good rather than
  correctly spelled. Does not resolve punctuation-in-context judgment calls
  (see 문장부호 sub-note below) beyond the codified symbol list. Does not
  govern journalistic, literary, or children's-audience register choices.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Pointer and our summary only. No rule text reproduced
  verbatim; our own restatement lives in the language pack.

### 국립국어원 표준국어대사전 (Standard Korean Dictionary)

- **Source**: 국립국어원, 표준국어대사전. https://stdict.korean.go.kr/main/main.do
- **Authority class**: NORMATIVE
- **Governs**: Which headwords, senses, and spellings count as standard
  Korean (표준어) — the prescriptive reference used in courts and official
  contexts per 국립국어원's own description of the dictionary's function.
- **Does NOT govern**: Living usage, emerging words, dialect, slang, or
  register-appropriateness for a given publication (that is a style
  decision, not a dictionary lookup). A word's presence does not make it
  appropriate for a given content type or audience.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only. We do not reproduce dictionary entries.

### 국립국어원 우리말샘 (Urimalsaem — open, participatory Korean dictionary)

- **Source**: 국립국어원, 우리말샘. https://opendict.korean.go.kr/main
- **Authority class**: EMPIRICAL_REFERENCE
- **Governs**: Nothing normative. It is descriptive lexicography — a record
  of neologisms, jargon, dialect, and colloquial usage as they are actually
  used, contributed and edited by the public and NIKL staff together.
- **Does NOT govern**: Correctness or standard status of any word. A term's
  presence in 우리말샘 is evidence the word exists in use, not evidence it is
  standard Korean or appropriate register. Never cite 우리말샘 to justify a
  spelling or usage the 어문 규범/표준국어대사전 does not support.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

### 국어기본법 (Framework Act on the Korean Language)

- **Source**: 대한민국 법률, 국어기본법 (문화체육관광부 소관).
  https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//lsBdyPrint.do?efYd=20170922&lsiSeq=192465&joNo=0003:00&chrClsCd=010202
- **Authority class**: NORMATIVE
- **Governs**: The legal basis for 어문규범 itself (Art. 13 국어심의회 심의 →
  문화체육관광부장관 고시) and the statutory requirement that 공공기관 write
  official documents in Hangul, in terms and sentences "일반 국민이 알기 쉬운"
  (easy for ordinary citizens to understand), conforming to 어문규범.
- **Does NOT govern**: Private or editorial publishing. The plain-language
  duty in this law binds 공공기관(public institutions), not us. It is cited
  here only as the legal root of the normative standards above and of the
  public-language guidance in §2 — not as authority over this publication's
  own house style.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Public statute text; link only, no reproduction of
  articles beyond the paraphrase above.

### 방송통신심의위원회 방송심의에 관한 규정 (Korea Communications Standards Commission — Broadcast Review Regulations)

- **Source**: 방송통신심의위원회, 방송심의에 관한 규정 (행정규칙).
  https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/admRulInfoP.do?admRulSeq=2200000076849
- **Authority class**: NORMATIVE
- **Governs**: Standard-language use by broadcast hosts, restrictions on
  dialect caricature, slang, and neologism in broadcast speech — a
  broadcasting-medium-specific normative regime.
- **Does NOT govern**: Print, digital, or editorial writing of any kind.
  This regulates a licensed broadcast medium we do not operate in; it is
  recorded here only because it is a real, checkable Korean-language
  authority, not because it applies to this system.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

## 2. Public / plain-Korean guidance

### 국립국어원 (개정판) 한눈에 알아보는 공공언어 바로 쓰기

- **Source**: 국립국어원, "(개정판) 한눈에 알아보는 공공언어 바로 쓰기."
  https://www.korean.go.kr/front/etcData/etcDataView.do?mn_id=&etc_seq=699&pageIndex=1
- **Authority class**: STRONG_GUIDANCE
- **Governs**: Plain-Korean practice for **공공언어** — documents addressed by
  government and public institutions to citizens: avoiding 번역투 (translation-ese),
  이중피동 (double passive), unnecessary 한자어/외래어, over-nominalization,
  and unclear sentence structure in official/administrative text.
- **Does NOT govern**: Editorial or literary Korean generally. This is
  **not** a universal style authority. It governs public-facing
  administrative and civic communication; it has no jurisdiction over an
  editorial publication's voice, argument structure, or register choices,
  even where its underlying diagnoses (over-nominalization, translation-ese)
  are also craft problems in editorial writing. Where the language pack
  borrows a diagnosis from this material, that borrowing is stated as our
  own adoption, not as this body's authority extending to us.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link + our notes only, no reproduction of its examples
  or checklists.

### 국립국어원 쉬운 공문서 쓰기 길잡이

- **Source**: 국립국어원, "쉬운 공문서 쓰기 길잡이" (조사 대상: 행정문서 표현
  개선 및 쉬운 공공언어 쓰기 지침 개발, 2021–2022 연구 기반).
  https://www.korean.go.kr/front/etcData/etcDataView.do?mn_id=&etc_seq=700&pageIndex=1
- **Authority class**: STRONG_GUIDANCE
- **Governs**: Same scope as above, narrowed to 공문서 (official administrative
  documents) specifically.
- **Does NOT govern**: Editorial writing, journalism, or any genre outside
  administrative documents. Same caveat as above applies with equal force.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

### 국립국어원 새말모임 / 다듬은 말 (Refined-word list)

- **Source**: 국립국어원, "다듬은 말" (새말모임, 2019– ; 통합 말다듬기
  위원회 이후). https://www.korean.go.kr/front/imprv/refineList.do
- **Authority class**: STRONG_GUIDANCE
- **Governs**: Officially proposed Korean replacements for newly imported
  foreign terms and loanwords (e.g., recent examples such as replacements for
  "부스터 숏", "오마카세", "무라벨"), arrived at through expert discussion,
  public-acceptance survey, and 국어심의회 resolution.
- **Does NOT govern**: Mandatory usage. These are recommended substitutions,
  not a ban on the source loanword; adoption in general usage (including
  journalism) is uneven and this list does not override a publication's own
  terminology decisions, especially for domain terms with an established
  Korean financial/technical usage (see §6).
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only; specific replacement pairs cited by name when
  relevant to a decision, not reproduced as a list.

## 3. Journalism / newsroom authorities

### 신문윤리강령 및 신문윤리실천요강 (Korean Newspaper Ethics Code and Code of Practice)

- **Source**: 한국신문협회·한국신문방송편집인협회·한국기자협회 공동 제정,
  한국신문윤리위원회 시행. https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/section4.html?p_num=21
- **Authority class**: STRONG_GUIDANCE
- **Governs**: Self-regulatory newsroom ethics for Korean newspapers and wire
  services — editorial independence, fairness, sourcing conduct, correction
  practice. An industry self-regulatory code, not law.
- **Does NOT govern**: Language mechanics (spelling, spacing, grammar) —
  it is an ethics code, not a style guide. Does not bind non-newspaper
  publications; we are not a member of this self-regulatory body and adopt
  nothing here as a compliance obligation, only as evidence of how Korean
  newsroom ethics are institutionally framed.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

### 한국기자협회 윤리강령 및 실천요강 (Journalists Association of Korea — Code of Ethics)

- **Source**: 한국기자협회. https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/section4.html?p_num=4
- **Authority class**: STRONG_GUIDANCE
- **Governs**: Individual reporter conduct — fair reporting, sourcing,
  independence from pressure, national/social responsibilities as stated in
  the code's preamble.
- **Does NOT govern**: Prose style or language mechanics. It is a
  professional-ethics code, not a writing standard. Non-binding on
  non-members.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

## 4. Report / institutional writing authorities

No Korean institution surfaced a distinct, published *report-writing style
guide* comparable to §2's public-language guidance. What we found instead is
each institution's own published output, which is empirical evidence of
report register and terminology use — not a codified standard for how to
write a report.

### 한국은행 (Bank of Korea) published reports

- **Source**: 한국은행, 경제전망보고서/통화신용정책보고서 and related
  publications. https://www.bok.or.kr (경제교육/뉴스자료 sections; specific
  report URLs vary by issue)
- **Authority class**: EMPIRICAL_REFERENCE
- **Governs**: Nothing normative. Evidence of how BOK phrases monetary-policy
  and macroeconomic analysis in Korean — sentence register, hedging language,
  standard phrasing for forecasts and risk statements.
- **Does NOT govern**: Correctness of Korean grammar or terminology choice
  outside what BOK itself uses; a BOK report's phrasing is not a rule, only
  an instance of institutional register we may study.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only; no reproduction of report text.

### 국회예산정책처 (National Assembly Budget Office, NABO) published reports

- **Source**: 국회예산정책처, 경제동향&이슈 등 정기간행물.
  https://www.nabo.go.kr/Sub/01Report/09_Board.jsp
- **Authority class**: EMPIRICAL_REFERENCE
- **Governs**: Same role as above — evidence of Korean fiscal/economic
  report register, not a style standard.
- **Does NOT govern**: Anything prescriptive.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

### KDI (한국개발연구원) published reports

- **Source**: KDI 한국개발연구원, research and forecast publications.
  https://www.kdi.re.kr
- **Authority class**: EMPIRICAL_REFERENCE
- **Governs**: Same role — evidence only.
- **Does NOT govern**: Anything prescriptive. We searched specifically for a
  KDI report-writing style/terminology guide and found none distinct from
  its research output; see Unverified section.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

### 통계청 (Statistics Korea, KOSTAT) 통계용어사전

- **Source**: 통계청, 통계용어사전 (사전식보기, 통계이해).
  https://kostat.go.kr/menu.es?mid=a10506000000
- **Authority class**: TERMINOLOGY_AUTHORITY
- **Governs**: Canonical Korean terms for statistical concepts and indicators
  as used in Statistics Korea's own publications.
- **Does NOT govern**: General report style. It is a term dictionary, not a
  writing guide.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

## 5. Audience / age / literacy authorities

### 국립국어원 한국어 학습용 어휘 목록 (2003, graded)

- **Source**: 국립국어연구원(현 국립국어원), "한국어 학습용 어휘 목록" (2003),
  3 등급(1단계 982어, 2단계 2,111어, 3단계 2,872어, 총 5,965어).
  https://www.korean.go.kr/front/etcData/etcDataView.do?mn_id=46&etc_seq=71
- **Authority class**: TERMINOLOGY_AUTHORITY
- **Governs**: A graded Korean vocabulary list originally built for
  Korean-as-a-foreign/second-language learners, usable as one proxy for
  "how basic is this word in Korean" when calibrating audience-level
  vocabulary — including for a general/young readership, not only L2
  learners.
- **Does NOT govern**: Native-speaker child reading level directly — it was
  built for adult L2 learners, not for age-graded native literacy. Using it
  for an audience such as 초등 고학년 (10–12) requires treating it as a proxy
  for lexical basicness, not as an age-calibrated instrument. It is
  vocabulary-level evidence, not readability-formula authority, and it says
  nothing about sentence length, syntax complexity, or genre.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only; word lists not reproduced.

### 국립국어원 국어 기초 어휘 목록 (2024, 5-tier, corpus-based)

- **Source**: 국립국어원, "국어 기초 어휘 선정 및 어휘 등급화" research
  (2017–2023, ~2억 어절 구어/문어 말뭉치 기반), released as a ~4만 word
  5-tier graded list in 2024.
  https://korean.go.kr/front/reportData/reportDataView.do?mn_id=207&searchOrder=years&report_seq=1080&pageIndex=1
- **Authority class**: TERMINOLOGY_AUTHORITY
- **Governs**: A corpus-derived, native-usage-based graded vocabulary list
  across 5 acquisition-order tiers — a stronger proxy than the 2003 list for
  "how early/common is this word in Korean," since it is built from
  actual national usage patterns rather than an L2 curriculum.
- **Does NOT govern**: It is not an age-of-reader instrument by itself —
  the tiers reflect acquisition/frequency order in the corpus, not a
  validated mapping to a specific age band such as "10–12-year-old readers."
  Applying it to that age band is our own interpretive step, to be stated
  as such wherever it is used, not attributed to 국립국어원 as an age
  standard.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

### Academic Korean-text readability-formula research (no single authoritative body)

- **Source**: Multiple individual academic papers (e.g., KCI-indexed studies
  on 한국어 텍스트 난이도 측정 공식, TOPIK reading-difficulty comparisons with
  native elementary students) found via KCI/RISS-adjacent search, not a
  single named institute or standard.
- **Authority class**: EMPIRICAL_REFERENCE
- **Governs**: Nothing institutionally binding. These are individual
  research results (regression-based difficulty formulas combining lexical
  and syntactic difficulty) — informative as method, not as a citable
  authority.
- **Does NOT govern**: There is no equivalent in Korean to a single
  canonical Flesch-Kincaid-style body for this purpose that we could verify.
  Treat any specific formula from this literature as EMPIRICAL_REFERENCE at
  most, never as NORMATIVE, and never cite "Korean readability research"
  as if it names one settled standard.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Not catalogued individually here; would require a
  per-paper `references/catalog.json` entry with its own DOI/URL before any
  specific formula is adopted.

## 6. Domain terminology authorities

### 한국은행 경제금융용어 700선

- **Source**: 한국은행, "경제금융용어 700선."
  https://www.bok.or.kr/portal/bbs/B0000249/view.do?nttId=235017&menuNo=200765
- **Authority class**: TERMINOLOGY_AUTHORITY
- **Governs**: Canonical Korean definitions and phrasing for ~700 core
  economic/financial terms tied to BOK's own policy domain (monetary policy,
  real economy, financial stability, payment systems).
- **Does NOT govern**: Financial terms outside BOK's domain focus (e.g.,
  capital-markets or corporate-finance-specific vocabulary is thinner here
  than at 금융감독원). Not a style guide; a glossary only.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only; no term-by-term reproduction beyond citing a
  specific term's presence.

### 금융감독원 금융용어사전 (FINE — 금융소비자 정보포털)

- **Source**: 금융감독원, 금융용어사전, hosted on 파인(FINE).
  https://fine.fss.or.kr/fine/fnctip/fncDicary/list.do?menuNo=900021
- **Authority class**: TERMINOLOGY_AUTHORITY
- **Governs**: Canonical, consumer-facing Korean definitions for ~530+
  financial terms across banking, insurance, capital markets, and fintech,
  maintained by the financial regulator.
- **Does NOT govern**: Term choice for editorial or analytical writing style
  — it defines terms, it does not prescribe how to use them in a sentence.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

### TTA 정보통신용어사전 (Telecommunications Technology Association — ICT Terminology Dictionary)

- **Source**: 한국정보통신기술협회(TTA), TTA정보통신용어사전.
  https://terms.tta.or.kr/dictionary/searchList.do
- **Authority class**: TERMINOLOGY_AUTHORITY
- **Governs**: Standardized Korean ICT/telecom/AI-adjacent technical
  terminology across telecommunications, broadcasting, computing, internet,
  and information security — open submission with internal review before a
  term is registered.
- **Does NOT govern**: General AI/technology journalism vocabulary choices
  where the everyday term differs from the standardized technical term (a
  common split in fast-moving AI terminology); does not prescribe register
  for a general-audience piece.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

### 국립국어원 새말모임/다듬은 말 for AI/technology terms

- **Source**: Same body as §2's 다듬은 말 entry; specifically its output on
  imported technology/AI terms.
  https://www.korean.go.kr/front/imprv/refineList.do
- **Authority class**: STRONG_GUIDANCE
- **Governs**: Recommended Korean substitutes for newly imported foreign
  tech/AI vocabulary, arrived at through the same expert+public-survey+국어심의회
  process as the general list.
- **Does NOT govern**: Mandatory adoption. See the caveat in §2 — a
  recommended substitute does not override established domain usage where
  the loanword is the term of record among practitioners.
- **Accessed**: 2026-09-05
- **Rights note**: Link only.

## Unverified / could not confirm

Honest gaps, kept rather than silently dropped.

- **A single "KOLAS"-style canonical Korean readability index.** Searched
  for a named, institutionally maintained Korean-language readability
  formula/authority equivalent to Flesch-Kincaid. Found only scattered
  individual academic papers (see §5) and no single maintained public index
  or issuing body. Do not cite "KOLAS" or an equivalent as if it is an
  established authority until a specific, checkable source is found.
- **A distinct KDI / 국회예산정책처 / 통계청 "report writing style guide."**
  Searched for published terminology or style guidance specifically about
  *how to write* institutional reports (as opposed to term glossaries or the
  reports themselves). Found budget/feasibility-study procedural guidelines
  (e.g., KDI 공공투자관리센터 예비타당성조사 지침) but nothing addressed to
  prose style or terminology-in-writing at the institutional level. Listed
  their actual reports in §4 as EMPIRICAL_REFERENCE instead — evidence of
  register, not a citable writing standard.
- **A named age-calibrated Korean vocabulary standard for "초등 고학년
  (10–12)" specifically.** The two 국립국어원 graded-vocabulary resources in
  §5 are real and verified, but neither is calibrated to that exact age
  band by 국립국어원 itself; any 10–12 mapping is our own interpretive
  layer, not an attributed institutional claim, and must be labeled as such
  wherever it is used.

## What this registry is not

- A pointer registry, nothing more. Every entry states what its source is
  authoritative *for* and, just as important, what it is not — authority
  here means scope, not correctness of any specific claim.
- Not the rules. The Korean language rules themselves — spacing, spelling,
  register, sentence construction, terminology choice — live in the Korean
  language pack, which is derived from these sources but is our own written
  artifact, not a reproduction of any one of them.
- Not fact authority. No entry in this file grants factual authority over
  an article's claims. A Source here answers "who governs this piece of
  Korean-language practice"; it never answers "is this claim true." Source
  and Reference are not the same thing, and this registry is exclusively the
  former.
